# Code of conduct

Aetox has one maintainer and a small room of people around it: people reporting bugs, asking questions,
sending corrections to documents, and publishing skills. This page is about how we talk to each other in
that room.

## What we expect

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This patch loses the file the user opened" is a contribution.
  "You clearly have no idea what you are doing" is not.
- **Say what you actually saw.** Version, what you did, what happened. A report that leaves out the log
  costs someone a round of questions.
- **Assume the other person is trying.** Most people who report something are being helpful, even when the
  report is wrong about the cause.
- **Give people room to be wrong in public.** Plenty of us have been confidently wrong about a stack trace.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or following someone from thread to thread.
- Attacks on a person's identity: nationality, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability,
  age, or how good their English or Thai is.
- Sexual attention or imagery where it was not asked for.
- Publishing someone's private information, including logs, screenshots or keys, without permission.
- Deliberately wasting people's time: false reports, repeated noise, or a bot filling the issue tracker.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion after being asked to stop.

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, the Facebook group, and any space where you are
representing the project.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour is the problem, do not argue it out in the thread. Open an issue titled only
**"Code of conduct — private channel requested"** with no details, and the maintainer will open a private
channel with you. Reports are read by the maintainer alone.

If what you are reporting is also a security problem, [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) is the faster door.

## What happens

- A first, smaller incident usually gets a private word and a request to stop.
- Anything that continues, or that is severe on its own — threats, doxxing, harassment — means removal
  from the project's spaces, without a warning first.
- The maintainer will not name the person who reported it, and will not discuss the details of a report
  with anyone else.

There is no appeal committee. There is one person who reads the whole thing and decides, and that is
the price of a project this size. Being told to stop is not a punishment; it is the request.

## Where this comes from

This page is written for this project rather than copied from a template, so it says what will actually
happen. Its shape owes a debt to the Contributor Covenant, which is the standard most projects build on.
